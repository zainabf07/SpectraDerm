SpectraDerm — Module J
Spectral Reconstruction Validation Report
==========================================

1. OBJECTIVE
------------

Module J validates whether the RGB-to-spectral reconstruction model produces
spectral outputs that are sufficiently consistent with measured hyperspectral
ground truth.

The validation focuses on:

- Predicted spectrum vs ground-truth spectrum
- MAE
- RMSE
- Spectral Angle Mapper (SAM)
- Per-band reconstruction quality
- Visual comparison of ground truth and prediction
- Identification of lower- and higher-error wavelength regions
- Suitability of the reconstructed spectra for downstream analysis

The purpose is to validate reconstruction quality before making any biological
or clinical interpretation of the reconstructed spectral information.


2. MODEL
--------

Model:
MST++ (MST_Plus_Plus)

Task:
RGB -> VIS spectral reconstruction

Input:
RGB image

Output:
31-band estimated VIS spectral cube

Spectral range:
400–700 nm

Spectral interval:
10 nm

Number of output bands:
31

The MST++ architecture was obtained from the Hyper-Skin project implementation.
The SpectraDerm team trained its own RGB-to-VIS MST++ model using Hyper-Skin
paired RGB/VIS data.

Checkpoint:
mstpp_hyperskin_vis_best_10pairs.pth

Important:
The reconstructed spectra are AI-estimated outputs and are not direct
spectrometer measurements.


3. VALIDATION DATA
------------------

Dataset:
Hyper-Skin

Held-out subjects evaluated:

- p035_neutral_front
- p036_smile_left
- p039_neutral_right

Evaluation procedure:

- RGB images were normalized to [0, 1].
- Corresponding VIS ground-truth cubes were loaded from the Hyper-Skin VIS
  data.
- A consistent 128 x 128 crop was used for each held-out subject.
- Each RGB crop was passed through the trained MST++ model.
- The resulting 31-band prediction was compared with the corresponding
  ground-truth VIS crop.

The same evaluation procedure was applied to all three subjects.

7. WAVELENGTH-DEPENDENT OBSERVATIONS
------------------------------------

The reconstruction quality is strongly wavelength-dependent.

Several bands showed relatively low reconstruction error across the three
held-out subjects, including:

- 410 nm
- 450 nm
- 530 nm
- 550 nm
- 580 nm

The lowest mean MAE was observed at:

530 nm:
Mean MAE = 0.010668

In contrast, reconstruction error increased substantially across much of
the 630–700 nm region.

The largest mean MAE values were observed at:

630 nm:
Mean MAE = 0.285385

650 nm:
Mean MAE = 0.282389

680 nm:
Mean MAE = 0.251040

700 nm:
Mean MAE = 0.242243

Therefore, the model should not be treated as having uniform reconstruction
quality across all 31 spectral bands.


8. VISUAL VALIDATION
--------------------

A visual comparison was performed between:

- RGB input
- Ground-truth spectral band
- MST++ predicted spectral band
- Absolute reconstruction error

A 550 nm example showed that the prediction preserved broad spatial facial
structure but was substantially smoother than the ground-truth spectral
image and showed visible reconstruction error.

A single-pixel spectral comparison was also performed. The initially selected
pixel was located in a dark non-skin region, so that comparison was not used
as evidence for skin reconstruction quality.

This highlighted an important validation consideration:

Pixel-level spectral interpretation should be performed on appropriate skin
regions rather than arbitrary image pixels.


9. INFERENCE ON A NEW RGB FACIAL IMAGE
--------------------------------------

The trained MST++ model was also tested on a new RGB facial image without
hyperspectral ground truth.

The image was resized while preserving its aspect ratio.

Input:
1536 x 1024 x 3 RGB

MST++ output:
31 x 1536 x 1024

The model successfully produced a complete 31-band estimated VIS spectral
cube.

Individual reconstructed wavelength images and false-color multispectral
composites were visualized.

This demonstrates that the trained model can perform RGB-to-spectral
inference on a new image.

However, because the new facial image does not have measured hyperspectral
ground truth, quantitative reconstruction accuracy cannot be calculated for
this image.


10. DOWNSTREAM-USE DECISION
---------------------------

Decision:
EXPERIMENTAL USE ONLY — NOT YET VALIDATED FOR BIOLOGICAL OR CLINICAL
INTERPRETATION.


The model successfully performs the intended technical transformation:

RGB image
    ->
MST++
    ->
31-band estimated VIS spectral cube

However, the three-subject validation demonstrates substantial
wavelength-dependent reconstruction error.

Therefore, the current evidence is not sufficient to treat the reconstructed
spectra as equivalent to measured hyperspectral data or to directly infer
biological skin properties.

The reconstructed spectral cube may be used for:

- Technical experimentation
- Spectral visualization
- Development and testing of downstream feature-extraction methods
- Investigation of whether reconstructed spectral patterns can support
  later analysis

It should NOT currently be used to claim:

- Clinical diagnosis
- Disease detection
- Actual hemoglobin concentration
- Actual melanin concentration
- Actual collagen concentration
- Clinically validated inflammation detection
- Clinically validated skin-condition detection


11. LIMITATIONS
---------------

1. Only three held-out subjects were evaluated in this Module J experiment.

2. Evaluation used a 128 x 128 crop from each held-out image rather than
   the complete images.

3. The selected crop strategy was fixed and may not represent all facial
   regions.

4. The validation dataset is relatively small for making broad
   generalization claims.

5. Reconstruction error varies considerably across wavelengths.

6. The reconstructed spectra are estimates generated by a neural network,
   not direct physical measurements.

7. No clinical ground-truth labels were used in this module.

8. No biological validation was performed in this module.

9. No predefined clinical acceptance threshold was established for MAE,
   RMSE, or SAM.

10. Therefore, the results should be interpreted as engineering/model
    validation rather than clinical validation.


12. CONCLUSION
--------------

Module J successfully validated the technical RGB-to-VIS spectral
reconstruction pipeline using paired Hyper-Skin ground-truth data.

The trained MST++ model successfully generated 31-band VIS spectral
outputs from RGB inputs.

Three held-out subjects produced the following mean results:

MAE  = 0.12756
RMSE = 0.14896
SAM  = 0.59420 radians
SAM  = 34.05 degrees

Per-band analysis demonstrated that reconstruction quality is not uniform
across the VIS spectrum. Several bands showed lower reconstruction error,
while substantial errors occurred particularly in the 630–700 nm region.

Consequently, the current reconstruction model is considered technically
functional and suitable for experimental downstream development, but the
available evidence is insufficient to treat the reconstructed spectral data
as clinically or biologically validated measurements.

Further validation and/or model improvement is required before using
reconstructed spectra as evidence for biological skin-feature inference.
