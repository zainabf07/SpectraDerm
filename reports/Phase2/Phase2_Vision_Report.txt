MODULE F — IMAGE QUALITY ASSESSMENT
-----------------------------------

Purpose:
    Assess whether an RGB image is sufficiently usable for downstream
    SpectraDerm processing.

Implemented quality dimensions:
    - Blur / sharpness
    - Mean exposure
    - Exposure clipping
    - Lighting uniformity
    - Resolution
    - Framing / visible content

Output:
    Quality status:
        GOOD
        REVIEW
        REJECT

Safety boundary:
    Image quality assessment is not diagnosis and does not establish
    disease presence or absence.

Testing:
    Automated tests: 15 passed.

Real-image validation:
    Hyper-Skin sample:
        p001_neutral_front.jpg
        Overall status: REVIEW

        Reasons:
            - Image may be somewhat blurred.
            - Image may be underexposed.
            - Lighting may be uneven.

    UMINHO-HSFD sample:
        207_RGB_pixelized.jpg
        Overall status: REVIEW

        Reasons:
            - Some pixels may be clipped.
            - Lighting may be uneven.

Blur calibration experiment:
    Controlled Gaussian degradation was applied to both datasets.

    Hyper-Skin:
        Original: 110.83
        Mild blur: 9.37
        Strong blur: 1.61

    UMINHO-HSFD:
        Original: 880.95
        Mild blur: 46.71
        Strong blur: 3.93

    Observation:
        The blur metric decreased consistently as controlled blur
        increased for both datasets.

Threshold status:
    Current blur thresholds are provisional engineering thresholds.
    They are NOT clinically validated and require broader calibration
    on a representative evaluation set before deployment.


MODULE G — SKIN DETECTION / SEGMENTATION
----------------------------------------

Purpose:
    Identify candidate skin pixels so downstream processing can focus
    on relevant skin regions.

Implementation:
    Explainable color-based baseline using:
        - YCrCb
        - HSV
        - normalized RGB features
        - morphological processing
        - connected-component filtering

Output:
    Binary skin mask
    Skin fraction
    Segmentation method metadata

Testing:
    Automated tests: 18 passed.

Real-image testing:

    Hyper-Skin:
        Skin fraction: 2.46%

    UMINHO-HSFD:
        Skin fraction: 81.14%

Observation:
    The baseline behaves substantially differently across the two
    datasets. Visual inspection showed that the Hyper-Skin baseline
    mask captures only small regions of the visible face.

Validation status:
    BASELINE IMPLEMENTED — NOT YET VALIDATED

    The current implementation must not be treated as a production
    quality skin segmentation model.

Safety boundary:
    Skin segmentation identifies candidate skin pixels only.
    It is not lesion detection, disease detection, or diagnosis.


MODULE H — REGION / ROI LOCALIZATION
------------------------------------

Purpose:
    Generate candidate regions of interest from the skin mask for
    downstream analysis and future longitudinal comparison.

Implementation:
    Connected-component analysis over the supplied skin mask.

Output:
    Candidate bounding boxes
    Candidate area
    Candidate score
    Localization method metadata

Testing:
    Automated tests: 26 total project tests passed after adding
    localization edge-case coverage.

Edge cases tested:
    - Empty masks
    - Multiple regions
    - Border-touching regions
    - Invalid numeric values
    - Invalid mask dimensions
    - Invalid minimum-area fractions

Real-image testing:

    Hyper-Skin:
        Skin fraction: 2.46%
        Candidate regions: 2

    UMINHO-HSFD:
        Skin fraction: 81.14%
        Candidate regions: 1
        Candidate bounding box:
            (0, 4, 615, 916)

Observation:
    The localization pipeline functions correctly, but the quality
    of its real-world ROIs depends directly on the quality of the
    upstream segmentation mask.

Validation status:
    IMPLEMENTED — BASELINE NOT YET ACCURACY-VALIDATED

Safety boundary:
    Candidate ROI localization does not identify a lesion or disease.
    Candidate regions must not be presented as diagnostic findings.
